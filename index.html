Every Weight a Verb Can Take

You already know how to weigh a verb. Now meet every weight it can take — the ten familiar forms of the trilateral verb (I–X), three rarer patterns used for intense qualities, and the matching family for 4-letter roots. Real verses from the Qur’an show the weights at work.

ثلاثي — I–X رباعي ١٠ حروف
Form I · Bare Trilateral

الفِعْلُ الثُّلَاثِيُّ المُجَرَّدEven the simplest verb has 3 faces

The vowel on the middle root letter is the only thing that changes.

فَعَلَ
كَتَبَ
fatḥa
he wrote
فَعِلَ
لَعِبَ
kasra
he played
فَعُلَ
كَرُمَ
ḍamma
he was generous
From the Qur’an — all three shapes
كَتَبَ رَبُّكُمْ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ الرَّحْمَةَ
فَعَلَ — Sūrah al-An‘ām, 6:54: “Your Lord has decreed mercy upon Himself.”
عَلِمَ اللَّهُ أَنَّكُمْ كُنتُمْ تَخْتَانُونَ أَنفُسَكُمْ
فَعِلَ — Sūrah al-Baqarah, 2:187: “Allah knew that you used to deceive yourselves.”
كَبُرَ مَقْتًا عِندَ اللَّهِ أَن تَقُولُوا مَا لَا تَفْعَلُونَ
فَعُلَ — Sūrah aṣ-Ṣaff, 61:3: “It is greatly hateful to Allah that you say what you do not do.”
i
Every other form is built by adding letters on top of one of these basic shapes.
Forms II, III, IV · One Added Letter

Double a letter, or add one

II
فَعَّلَ
قَتَّلَ
he killed violently/repeatedly — doubles the 2nd root letter
III
فَاعَلَ
كَاتَبَ
he corresponded with — inserts ا after the 1st root letter
IV
أَفْعَلَ
أَشْهَدَ
he made [someone] witness — prefixes a hamza
From the Qur’an
عَلَّمَ الْقُرْآنَ
Form II — Sūrah ar-Raḥmān, 55:2: “He taught the Qur’an.”
قَاتَلَ مَعَهُ رِبِّيُّونَ كَثِيرٌ
Form III — Sūrah Āl ‘Imrān, 3:146: “…alongside whom many devoted men fought.”
وَأَشْهَدَهُمْ عَلَىٰ أَنفُسِهِمْ
Form IV — Sūrah al-A‘rāf, 7:172: “and made them testify about themselves” — the exact verb أَشْهَدَ.
Forms V & VI · Add a ت to II & III

A ت in front changes the direction

V
تَفَعَّلَ
تَعَلَّمَ
he learned — Form II + تَ
VI
تَفَاعَلَ
تَبَادَلَ
they exchanged [with each other] — Form III + تَ
From the Qur’an
فَيَتَعَلَّمُونَ مِنْهُمَا مَا يُفَرِّقُونَ بِهِ بَيْنَ الْمَرْءِ وَزَوْجِهِ
Form V — Sūrah al-Baqarah, 2:102: “and they learn from them what separates a man from his wife” — the same root as تَعَلَّمَ.
وَتَوَلَّىٰ عَنْهُمْ
Form V — Sūrah Yūsuf, 12:84: “and he turned away from them.”
تَبَارَكَ الَّذِي بِيَدِهِ الْمُلْكُ
Form VI — Sūrah al-Mulk, 67:1: “Blessed is He in whose hand is dominion.” Same weight as تَبَادَلَ, different root.
i
Adding تَ to II or III often turns the action back toward the doer — reflexive, mutual, or gradual.
Forms VII & VIII · Two More Shapes

A hamzat waṣl opens two new patterns

VII
انْفَعَلَ
انْكَسَرَ
it broke — a ن added in front, often with a passive-like sense
VIII
افْتَعَلَ
اجْتَمَعَ
he gathered / they assembled — a ت inserted after the 1st root letter
From the Qur’an
وَإِذَا النُّجُومُ انكَدَرَتْ
Form VII — Sūrah at-Takwīr, 81:2: “and when the stars fall and scatter.” Same weight as انْكَسَرَ, different root.
قُل لَّئِنِ اجْتَمَعَتِ الْإِنسُ وَالْجِنُّ
Form VIII — Sūrah al-Isrā’, 17:88: “Say: if mankind and the jinn were to gather…” — the same verb as اجْتَمَعَ.
اقْتَرَبَ لِلنَّاسِ حِسَابُهُمْ
Form VIII — Sūrah al-Anbiyā’, 21:1: “The people’s reckoning has drawn near.”
Form IX · A Special Job

The verb form made for colors & defects

IX
افْعَلَّ
احْمَرَّ
ابْيَضَّ
it turned red · it turned white — doubles the last root letter
From the Qur’an
وَابْيَضَّتْ عَيْنَاهُ مِنَ الْحُزْنِ
Sūrah Yūsuf, 12:84: “and his eyes turned white from grief.”
يَوْمَ تَبْيَضُّ وُجُوهٌ وَتَسْوَدُّ وُجُوهٌ
Sūrah Āl ‘Imrān, 3:106: “On the Day some faces will turn white and some faces will turn black.”
✓
Recognize these? They're built from أَبْيَضُ، أَحْمَرُ، أَسْوَدُ — the colour adjectives from your Colors & Descriptions lesson. Form IX turns “white” the adjective into “to turn white” the verb.

rare

From the Qur’an — Form X
وَاسْتَغْفِرُوا اللَّهَ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ
Sūrah al-Baqarah, 2:199: “and seek Allah’s forgiveness; indeed Allah is Forgiving and Merciful.”
i
The three dashed patterns mostly intensify a quality or colour in classical, literary Arabic. I don't know of a verse that uses them, so no Qur’anic example is shown for these three.

4-letter roots · Part 1

الفِعْلُ الرُّبَاعِيّBare, and augmented by one letter

A 4-letter root gets its own pattern family, parallel to everything you just saw.

فَعْلَلَ
زَلْزَلَ
to shake — bare
فَعْلَلَ
حَرْجَمَ
to crowd together — bare
تَفَعْلَلَ
تَدَحْرَجَ
it rolled — +تَ (one letter added)
From the Qur’an — the bare pattern
فَوَسْوَسَ لَهُمَا الشَّيْطَانُ
فَعْلَلَ — Sūrah al-A‘rāf, 7:20: “then Satan whispered to them.”
إِذَا زُلْزِلَتِ الْأَرْضُ زِلْزَالَهَا
Sūrah az-Zalzalah, 99:1: “When the earth is shaken with its [final] quake” — the passive of زَلْزَلَ.
i
I don't know of a verse using the augmented pattern تَفَعْلَلَ, so none is shown for it.
4-letter roots · Part 2

مَزِيدُ الرُّبَاعِيّAugmented by two letters — two patterns

افْعَنْلَلَ
احْرَنْجَمَ
to crowd / flock together — +ا and a ن inserted
افْعَلَلَّ
اسْبَطَرَّ
to stretch out at length — +ا and a doubled last letter
From the Qur’an — the افْعَلَلَّ pattern
اشْمَأَزَّتْ قُلُوبُ الَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِالْآخِرَةِ
Sūrah az-Zumar, 39:45: “the hearts of those who do not believe in the Hereafter shrink in aversion.”
فَإِنْ أَصَابَهُ خَيْرٌ اطْمَأَنَّ بِهِ
Sūrah al-Ḥajj, 22:11: “if good befalls him, he is reassured by it.”
i
Both verbs fit افْعَلَلَّ — the same weight as اسْبَطَرَّ. I don't know of a verse using افْعَنْلَلَ.
